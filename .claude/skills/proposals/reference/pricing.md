# Pricing against the stated scope

Read the post's budget, the scope, and the client's history (total spend, hires, average hourly, the size of their recent fixed-price jobs). Then pick one case. The price line in the letter never apologises and never comments on their budget.

## Case 1: the budget fits the scope

Quote inside it, at or just under their figure, and say what it covers in one line. Where the work splits naturally, offer it as two milestones so the first one is easy to fund.

## Case 2: a real small budget

The client's history shows small jobs and modest spend, and the scope is bigger than the figure. Scope the work to fit the budget: name the one thing that gets done for that amount and what it leaves for later. The client gets a true price for a true piece of work, not a shrunk version of the whole.

## Case 3: a placeholder budget

The figure can't cover what they want, and the client's history shows they can carry more: large total spend, many hires, hourly bills past the posted figure, "willing to pay more for the right person", an expert-tier post. Treat the figure as a placeholder. Quote what the whole job will cost, split into milestones with the first one at or near their figure (usually the audit or the first measurable piece). No comment on the budget, no apology. Shrinking a big scope to a placeholder number undersells the work and sets up a dispute later.

At the approval gate, name the spend signals that put it in case 3, and flag the risk if their recent fixed-price jobs all sit near the posted figure (they may really be a case 2).

## Hourly posts

Quote the user's rate from `context/offer.md`, with an estimate of hours as a range tied to the plan steps. Never a rate the user hasn't set.

## Milestones

Three at most for a first contract. Each one has a name the client understands, a deliverable they can see, and a number. The first is the one they can say yes to today.

## Contract titles

When a contract is likely, suggest a title: 50 characters max (count with `wc -m`), phrased as a keyword-rich title that reads well on a profile's work history (platform + skill + deliverable, e.g. "WordPress On-Page SEO Content & Menu Update"), not a plain description of the task.

## Never

- A price in a cold email (that's the outreach skill; no price, no timeline there).
- A discount offered unasked.
- A number the user hasn't agreed to; when in doubt, show the reasoning and let them set it.
